# Promoting Trust and Professionalism in an Era of Digital Health and (mis)Information

**Friday, 2026-09-18** · 8:30 AM - 9:30 AM CST

**Topic:** [[Board Certification and Professionalism]]

## Speakers

- [[Wendy Nickel, MPH (she-her-hers)]] — Chief Program Officer, The University City Science Center, Pennsylvania
- [[Donald J. Palmisano, Jr., JD (he-him-his)]] — CEO, American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy, Illinois
- [[Furman S. McDonald, MD, MPH (he-him-his)]] — President and Chief Executive Officer, American Board of Internal Medicine, Pennsylvania

## Description

The rapid expansion of digitally driven care—telehealth visits, algorithmic decision support, patient portals, wearable data streams—has reshaped how patients encounter the healthcare system, often without reshaping the relational foundations that sustain trust. At the same time, health information now circulates widely across digital platforms, exposing patients to a mix of credible guidance, opinion, and misinformation. This is a symptom of eroding institutional trust.

When confidence in institutions and expertise weakens, conflicting narratives gain traction and the shared understanding that underpins the patient–physician relationship can fray. Without intentional transparency, digital literacy, and reaffirmation of professional accountability, technology and misinformation together threaten to erode the relational trust that has long been central to high-quality care.

Paradoxically, a recent survey indicated that board certification was important to 93% of respondents and was also one of the top factors contributing to choice of physician. The respondents also believed that board certified physicians provide higher quality care than physicians who are not certified.

Board certification can promote trust in the patient-clinician relationship by ensuring clinical judgment remains primary, even when technology is involved. In an era when patients encounter conflicting health information and digitally delivered care, certification serves as validation that a physician has met rigorous training requirements and continues to demonstrate clinical knowledge and expertise. Board certification has the ability to restore trust and professionalism in a digital, information-rich care environment.Learning Objectives: Upon completion, participants will be able to examine how digitally mediated care and technology integration are reshaping the moral and relational foundations of the patient–physician relationship.Upon completion, participants will be able to explain how health misinformation undermines professional authority, shared decision-making, and trust in clinical encounters.Upon completion, participants will be able to describe how board certification is valued by patients and can serve as a foundation for promoting trust and professionalism in a digital and information-rich healthcare environment.
