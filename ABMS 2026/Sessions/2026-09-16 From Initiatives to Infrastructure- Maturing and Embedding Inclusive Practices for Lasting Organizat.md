# From Initiatives to Infrastructure: Maturing and Embedding Inclusive Practices for Lasting Organizational Impact

**Wednesday, 2026-09-16** · 3:00 PM - 4:00 PM CST

**Topic:** [[Emerging Topics]]

## Speakers

- [[Laura Castillo-Page, PhD]] — Chief People and Culture Officer, American Board of Medical Specialties
- [[Lorna Lynn, MD]] — VP, Medical Assessment Engagement; Interim Chief Equity and Inclusion Officer, American Board of Internal Medicine, Pennsylvania
- [[Adanwimo Okafor, JD]] — General Counsel and Chief Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion Officer, American Board of Surgery
- [[Gary L. Leroy, MD, FAAFP (he-him-his)]] — Senior Vice President for Diplomate Experience, American Board of Family Medicine, Kentucky

## Description

Medical certifying boards play a critical role in upholding standards of excellence, public trust, and professional accountability. Strengthening organizational culture, particularly around collaboration, transparency, and engagement, can enhance fairness and reinforce confidence in the certification process.

Historically, organizations have kick-started their culture change initiatives with trainings, diversity workshops, awareness programs, and celebratory events. These programmatic activities are important early steps in raising awareness and building momentum. However, research and practice-based experiences show that to achieve lasting culture change and measurable progress, these efforts must mature beyond isolated programs.

In other words, organizations need to shift from ad-hoc initiatives to systemic, long-term strategies that are embedded throughout the organization to build resilient and sustainable programs.

This session will highlight why moving beyond this stage is both challenging and crucial, as well as explore how to make that critical shift and why it’s essential for sustainable organizational outcomes. Drawing from organizational psychology and business management literature, we’ll examine how inclusive practices—when integrated into systems such as hiring, evaluation, and governance—can support long-term performance and resilience. By cultivating psychological safety and equitable opportunities for all employees, leaders can ensure longevity and development.

Participants will also discuss case studies of organizations that have matured their programs and have made progress in successfully demonstrating that true integration drives better results and lasting impact.Learning Objectives: Describe the evolution from programmatic to systemic equity and inclusion efforts, explaining why trainings and one-off programs alone often fall short, and how embedding these efforts into organizational systems leads to more sustainable culture change and business impact.Identify at least two organizational systems or processes that can be strengthened to foster transparency, engagement, and fairness—such as metrics development and data tracking, recruitment and hiring practices, or strategic planning.Apply frameworks and best practices for embedding inclusive values into organizational infrastructure, including leadership accountability, governance structures, and day-to-day operations, thereby ensuring that these initiatives center psychological safety and equitable opportunities for all employees and are aligned with and embedded into the overall business strategy.
