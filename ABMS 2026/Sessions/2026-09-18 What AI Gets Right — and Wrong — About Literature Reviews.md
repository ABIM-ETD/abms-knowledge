# What AI Gets Right — and Wrong — About Literature Reviews

**Friday, 2026-09-18** · 11:00 AM - 12:00 PM CST

**Topic:** [[Artificial Intelligence]]

## Speakers

- [[Rachel A. Knapp, MLIS (she-her-hers)]] — Information and Data Research Manager, ABMS Research and Education Foundation, Illinois
- [[Martin V. Pusic, MD, PhD (he-him-his)]] — Director, ABMS Research and Education Foundation, Massachusetts

## Description

Artificial intelligence tools are increasingly used to search, summarize, and synthesize medical literature, but their outputs can introduce both subtle and substantial risks.

This session uses a series of case studies to explore common pitfalls encountered when AI is used in the reading and writing of literature reviews for medical education and for certification. The presentation will focus on examining how AI can support targeted literature reviews, rather than its use in automating or conducting systematic reviews.

Each case study presents both a cautionary scenario, such as fabricated citations, oversimplified evidence synthesis, or invisible bias, followed by practical beneficial suggestions for responsibly harnessing AI in those situations.

Participants will leave with concrete techniques for evaluating AI generated content, strengthening transparency, and integrating AI into literature review workflows without compromising rigor, trust, or educational integrity.

AI does not replace the usual literature review process, but it reshapes where human judgment matters most. When used thoughtfully, AI can expose literature gaps, accelerate screening, and support transparency. When used carelessly, it can quietly erode rigor.Learning Objectives: Identify common risks associated with the use of AI in literature review work, including fabricated citations, oversimplified evidence synthesis, and invisible bias.Evaluate AI-generated literature summaries and citations by assessing accuracy, source traceability, and completeness in relation to the underlying evidence.
