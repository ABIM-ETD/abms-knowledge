# Defining Informatics Competence for an AI-Enabled Clinical Workforce: Early Insights From Surgical CBME

**Thursday, 2026-09-17** · 3:00 PM - 4:00 PM CST

**Topic:** [[Artificial Intelligence]], [[Physician Workforce]]

## Speakers

- [[Phillip D. Jenkins, MD]] — Resident, Oregon Health & Science University, Oregon
- [[Ruchi Thanawala, MD, MS]] — Associate Professor, Oregon Health & Science University, Oregon
- [[Julie Doberne, MD, PhD (she-her-hers)]] — Assistant Professor, Oregon Health & Science University, Oregon

## Description

As AI-enabled systems increasingly influence documentation, decision support, and quality measurement, clinicians must possess informatics competencies to engage with these tools safely, transparently, and responsibly. However, informatics expectations for physicians remain poorly defined and inconsistently embedded within existing competency frameworks, milestones, and certification standards. Compounding this challenge is a parallel lack of informatics expertise in the workforce; of the over one million board certified physicians in the US, only 3000 (0.3%) are board certified informaticians.

CBME provides a natural structure for addressing this gap, yet few specialties have made informatics competence explicit within CBME implementation. Surgical training represents an early and mature model of CBME adoption, with widespread use of EPAs and digitally mediated assessment workflows. Leveraging this environment, our group has deployed educational content, examined how informatics-related skills are implicitly required, variably demonstrated, and inconsistently assessed across established surgical training and educational pathways.

During this work, we have identified recurring informatics domains essential for effective participation in CBME and contemporary clinical practice, including data interpretation, digital workflow navigation, assessment documentation quality, and longitudinal performance analysis. Building on surgery as an initial model, we present a scalable, specialty-agnostic approach to embed informatics competence into CBME and certification frameworks, supporting a physician workforce prepared to practice in an AI-enabled healthcare environment.Learning Objectives: Identify the core informatics and artificial intelligence competencies required for contemporary clinical practice using a competency-based medical education framework.Describe actionable assessment strategies capable of evaluating clinical informatics and artificial intelligence competence using workplace-based and EPA-aligned evidence.Evaluate pathways for integrating Clinical Informatics training into existing training programs that prepare the clinician for artificial intelligence-enabled health systems.
