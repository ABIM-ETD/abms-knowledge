# Using LLMs to Score ABS Oral Certifying Exams: A Pilot Study at The American Board of Surgery

**Thursday, 2026-09-17** · 4:30 PM - 5:30 PM CST

**Topic:** [[Artificial Intelligence]]

## Speakers

- [[Andrew T. Jones, PhD (he-him-his)]] — Chief Operating and Assessment Officer, American Board of Surgery, Pennsylvania
- [[Carol L. Barry, PhD (she-her-hers)]] — Director of Psychometrics and Research, American Board of Surgery, Pennsylvania
- [[Caroline O. Prendergast, PhD (she-her-hers)]] — Psychometrician, American Board of Surgery
- [[Mike Gartley]] — Director of Information Technology, American Board of Surgery, Florida

## Description

The recent development of widely available large-language models (LLMs) has given medical certification organizations the opportunity to experiment with making significant changes and potential enhancements to their assessment programs. The American Board of Surgery has launched a pilot to use LLMs to analyze its oral certifying exam (CE).

This session will give an overview of the pilot and lessons learned from using LLMs to analyze CE data. Topics covered will include operational, technological, psychometric, and ethical concerns.

Specifically, the session will cover:

The overall goals and framework for the pilot
Technical configuration and security considerations
Model training and results for scoring
Model training and results for examiner feedback
Ethical considerations for LLM scoring
Open questions and future considerations
These lessons could also be applied to organizations using OSCEs or standardized patients, or that are interested in scoring open-ended data for their exams. Results of this pilot have potential applications in both high-stakes summative decision making as well as formative feedback for examiners who deliver oral exams.
