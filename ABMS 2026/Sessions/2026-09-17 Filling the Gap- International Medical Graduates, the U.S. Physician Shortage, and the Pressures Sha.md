# Filling the Gap: International Medical Graduates, the U.S. Physician Shortage, and the Pressures Shaping the Pipeline

**Thursday, 2026-09-17** · 3:00 PM - 4:00 PM CST

**Topic:** [[Physician Workforce]]

## Speakers

- [[Tracy Wallowicz, MLS (she-her-hers)]] — Sr. VP, Regulatory Affairs, and Chief Communications Officer, Intealth, Pennsylvania
- [[Elizabeth Ingraham]] — AVP, External Relations & Public Affairs, Intealth, Pennsylvania

## Description

The United States does not produce enough physicians domestically to fill its residency programs. The gap between U.S. medical school graduates and available residency positions has been filled for many years by international medical graduates (IMGs), both U.S. citizens who were educated abroad and foreign nationals. Comprising roughly 25% of active physicians, IMGs are not a supplement to the domestic supply; they are a structural necessity, and disproportionately so in primary care and in rural, urban, and underserved communities.

Understanding what threatens this pipeline is urgent. This presentation opens with a data-driven profile of IMG contributions, with focus on medically underserved areas where unmet need is most acute. From that foundation, presenters examine contemporary developments with real potential to impact the IMG pipeline, both positively and negatively: significant changes in U.S. immigration policy and visa processing; the growing number of states creating alternative licensing pathways for internationally trained physicians; and the continuing shortfall in domestic medical school capacity relative to available residency positions. Together these forces shape how many physicians are available to train in and serve U.S. communities.

The presentation draws on data and insights from Intealth’s decades of experience through ECFMG Certification, primary-source credential verification, J-1 visa sponsorship, and research on IMG distribution in U.S. health care.Learning Objectives: Upon completion, participants will be able to describe the structural gap between U.S. physician production and residency demand, and the role IMGs play in filling that gap.Upon completion, participants will be able to identify key developments that affect the IMG pipeline and the availability of physicians to train in and serve U.S. communities.Upon completion, participants will be able to access data on IMG contributions and apply these data to articulate the consequences of pipeline disruptions for access to care.
