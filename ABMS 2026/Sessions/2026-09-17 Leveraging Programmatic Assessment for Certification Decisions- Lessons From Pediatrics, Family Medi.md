# Leveraging Programmatic Assessment for Certification Decisions: Lessons From Pediatrics, Family Medicine, and Surgery

**Thursday, 2026-09-17** · 4:30 PM - 5:30 PM CST

**Topic:** [[Research and Innovations]]

## Speakers

- [[Ariel Winn, MD]] — Assistant Professor, Associate Designated Institutional Official, Boston Children's Hospital/Harvard Medical School, Massachusetts
- [[Jacob A. Greenberg, MD, EdM (he-him-his)]] — Vice Chair of Education, Dept of Surgery, Duke University School of Medicine, North Carolina
- [[Warren P. Newton, MD MPH (he-him-his)]] — President and CEO, American Board of Family Medicine, Kentucky

## Description

Training programs, often as a result of ABMS Member Board innovation, are increasingly implementing programs of assessment informed by competency-based medical education (CBME) that can be leveraged to make competency-based certification decisions.

This session brings together three Member Boards on the leading edge of this work to share their approaches, rationale, findings, and lessons learned.

First, a leader from Surgery will describe pioneering national implementation of a smartphone-based frontline assessment platform across 300+ programs, its impact on Clinical Competency Committee decision-making, and plans for integrating resulting data into certification decisions.

Second, a leader from Pediatrics will describe the process by which they adapted similar technology for pediatrics, with a focus on how they ensured that the resulting platform and all aspects of programmatic assessment leading to program director attestations informing certification decisions are grounded in a clear and unambiguous shared construct of what it means to be practice ready.

Third, a leader from Family Medicine will describe their approach, named Competency-Based Board Eligibility, which includes equipping programs with a comprehensive data system and specialty-wide faculty development.

Finally, a panel discussion will explore similarities and differences across approaches and offer actionable guidance for other specialties.Learning Objectives: Describe how three member boards are leveraging programmatic assessment to make competency-based certification decisions.Compare three distinct approaches to integrating programmatic assessment into certification, identifying similarities and differences in rationale and design.Identify lessons learned across specialties to inform implementation of programmatic assessment for certification in their own board or program.
