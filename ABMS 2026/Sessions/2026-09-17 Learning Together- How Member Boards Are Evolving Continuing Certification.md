# Learning Together: How Member Boards Are Evolving Continuing Certification

**Thursday, 2026-09-17** · 9:15 AM - 10:15 AM CST

**Topic:** [[Emerging Topics]], [[Research and Innovations]]

## Speakers

- [[David B. Joseph, MD]] — Urology EMBR/ past 3C Chair, American Board of Urology, Alabama
- [[Barbara Sampson, MD (she-her-hers)]] — Chair - 3C, American Board of Pathology, Florida
- [[Amy Young, MD (she-her-hers)]] — Executive Director, American Board of Obstetrics and Gynecology, Texas
- [[Carolyn Kinney, MD (she-her-hers)]] — Executive Director, American Board of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation, Arizona
- [[Laura Couch (she-her-hers)]] — Program Manager, American Board of Pediatrics, North Carolina
- [[Mary S. Taylor-Blasi (she-her-hers)]] — Program Manager, Certification Standards and Programs, American Board of Medical Specialties, Illinois

## Description

This panel will bring together three to four ABMS Member Boards, each reviewing how community learning and lessons learned help to identify new areas in Member Board continuing certification programs, enhancing compliance of the Standards for Continuing Certification.

Each board will discuss how they were influenced by the learning community of the Continuing Certification Committee (3C) and how they crafted their program by increasing their diplomate experience through education and new opportunities for quality improvement initiatives. Creating a dialogue with stakeholders, especially engagement of diplomates is an important component for program success.

Member Boards presenting include the American Board of Urology (ABU), American Board of Obstetrics and Gynecology (ABOG), American Board of Pediatrics (ABPeds), and possibly one other board.Learning Objectives: List opportunities for improvement of MB continuing certification programs.Understand how a learning community promotes new opportunities for improvement in continuing certification.Identify areas in continuing certification programs that enhance the education of diplomates: appreciate the benefit of engaging diplomates in the process of improvement.
