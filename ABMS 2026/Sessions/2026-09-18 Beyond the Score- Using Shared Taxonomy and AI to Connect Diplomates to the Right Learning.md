# Beyond the Score: Using Shared Taxonomy and AI to Connect Diplomates to the Right Learning

**Friday, 2026-09-18** · 11:00 AM - 12:00 PM CST

**Topic:** [[Research and Innovations]]

## Speakers

- [[Zak Barbezat (he-him-his)]] — Software Engineer, American Board of Internal Medicine, Pennsylvania
- [[Jim McAdams, MBA (he-him-his)]] — Senior Director, Innovation Strategy and Solutions, American Board of Internal Medicine, Pennsylvania

## Description

Certification exams generate rich data about what physicians know — and where their knowledge gaps lie. Yet most diplomates receive only a score report or progress report. That report tells them how they did. It does not tell them what to do next.

ABIM's work on individualized learning uncovered two interconnected gaps. First, diplomates could benefit from richer, more actionable performance insights — not just item-level results, but patterns across time and topic areas that point toward specific learning needs. Second, even when a diplomate identifies a gap, they face a plethora of society resources with no clear map from "what I need to learn" to "where I should go."

This session presents a two-part solution developed in partnership with medical specialty societies. The first is an AI-driven feedback system that surfaces learning trends beyond scores — identifying patterns that diplomates and their advisors can act on. The second is a shared taxonomy: a common language co-developed between ABIM and societies that links what certification measures to what societies teach.

Together, these tools create a connected pathway: from assessment data, to personalized insight, to targeted learning resources. Attendees will see how this model works in practice and leave with a framework for building similar data partnerships between certification programs and the societies that serve their diplomates.Learning Objectives: Identify performance insight beyond item-level scores that certification data can provide to help diplomates better understand their learning needs.Describe how a shared taxonomy of topic areas, co-developed between a testing organization and medical specialty societies, improves the learner journey.Apply a framework for building or improving collaborative data partnerships between certification programs and specialty societies.
