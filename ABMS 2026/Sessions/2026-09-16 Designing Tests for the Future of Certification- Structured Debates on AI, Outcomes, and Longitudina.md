# Designing Tests for the Future of Certification: Structured Debates on AI, Outcomes, and Longitudinal Assessment

**Wednesday, 2026-09-16** · 1:30 PM - 2:30 PM CST

**Topic:** [[Research and Innovations]]

## Speakers

- [[Sarah D. Schnabel, PhD (she-her-hers)]] — Psychometric Consultant, Sarah Schnabel Consulting, LLC, Illinois
- [[Andrew T. Jones, PhD (he-him-his)]] — Chief Operating and Assessment Officer, American Board of Surgery, Pennsylvania
- [[Mikaela M. Raddatz, PhD (she-her-hers)]] — Director of Psychometrics and Research, American Board of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation, Minnesota
- [[Susan Davis-Becker]] — Founding Partner, ACS Ventures, LLC, Nebraska

## Description

As certification programs confront rapid advances in artificial intelligence, evolving expectations of accountability, and increasing pressure to demonstrate relevance, long-standing assumptions about assessment are being challenged. This 60-minute interactive session brings together four measurement experts, two from ABMS member boards and two external experts, for a structured, debate-style exploration of how certification and continuing certification will evolve.

The session will focus on three core debate topics: (1) whether “knowledge” assessment should change in the age of AI; (2) whether patient outcomes are defensible measures of individual physician competence; and (3) the appropriate level of content customization in high-stakes certification and longitudinal assessment programs.

For each topic, panelists will present contrasting viewpoints in a moderated debate format, followed by a brief “clock-off” dialogue to surface nuance, tradeoffs, and areas of convergence. Attendees will be invited to vote on positions and propose additional questions for discussion. The session is designed to provoke thoughtful discussion, challenge default assumptions, and equip leaders to make informed, strategic decisions about innovation in assessment design.Learning Objectives: Evaluate competing perspectives on how artificial intelligence should influence the definition and assessment of physician knowledge in initial and continuing certification.Assess the strengths and limitations of using patient outcomes (including case logs) as indicators of individual physician competence in initial and continuing certification contexts.Identify key tradeoffs associated with increased assessment content customization in certification programs and their implications for validity, fairness, and public trust.
