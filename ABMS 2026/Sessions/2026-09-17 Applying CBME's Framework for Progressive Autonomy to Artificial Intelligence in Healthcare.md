# Applying CBME's Framework for Progressive Autonomy to Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare

**Thursday, 2026-09-17** · 3:00 PM - 4:00 PM CST

**Topic:** [[Artificial Intelligence]], [[Research and Innovations]]

- [[Laura Chiel, MD, MHPE]] — Associate Program Director, Boston Children's Hospital
- [[Ting Wang, PhD (she-her-hers)]] — Director of Assessment Innovation Research, American Board of Pediatrics, North Carolina
- [[David A. Turner, MD (he-him-his)]] — Vice President, Innovation and Competency-Based Education, American Board of Pediatrics, North Carolina

## Description

Competency-based medical education (CBME) was built to answer a single question: how do we grant progressive autonomy to a powerful, imperfect, developing entity in service of the patient? Traditionally, that entity was always a trainee. Now there is a new entity – artificial intelligence (AI). As providers and health systems increasingly accept, rely on, and cede autonomy to AI in clinical care, the infrastructure for deciding how much autonomy, under what conditions, and with what oversight has not kept pace. Through CBME, the field of medical education has spent decades building exactly this infrastructure for physician trainees. The same framework and its lessons apply directly to AI.

This session maps key principles of CBME onto AI in healthcare: (1) Outcomes defined by patient and societal needs as the starting point for what gets built, funded, and prioritized. (2) Competency frameworks that define what a trustworthy entity looks like before granting autonomy. (3) Entrustment decisions calibrated to demonstrated competence, context, and risk. (4) Programmatic assessment through structures that aggregate individual judgments into system-level decisions. (5) Governance architecture (training programs, accrediting bodies, certifying bodies) that ensures accountability to the public.Learning Objectives: Map key principles of CBME onto AI in healthcare, identifying how each addresses a gap in current approaches to clinical AI deployment.Apply the concept of entrustment-based decision-making to clinical AI scenarios, calibrating progressive autonomy to demonstrated competence, context, and risk.Propose governance structures for clinical AI modeled on existing CBME infrastructure, including competency committees and certification bodies.
