# Unlocking Global Talent: Illinois’ Prescription for the Physician Workforce Shortage

## Description

The physician workforce in the United States (US) is struggling to keep pace with rapidly growing healthcare demands. Aging populations and expanding healthcare needs are major factors contributing to the physician shortage. As demand rises, internationally trained medical graduates (IMGs) represent an important and often underutilized resource.

IMGs make up roughly one quarter of the active physician workforce and are more likely than US-trained physicians to practice in medically underserved areas. Despite their contributions, IMGs often face significant barriers, including complex licensing requirements, residency placement challenges, visa restrictions, and differences in medical education systems. Many highly qualified physicians must repeat training or spend years navigating certification processes before they can practice in the US.

Illinois is among the first states to introduce policies focused on alternative pathways to integrate IMGs into the US healthcare workforce. These pathways maintain consistent standards of medical knowledge, clinical competence, and patient safety while allowing qualified IMGs to enter practice more efficiently.

Traditional licensing typically requires IMGs to repeat residency training even if they have already practiced for years in their home countries. Alternative licensing pathways aim to better utilize this global talent while maintaining high standards of care.
